Vaporwave Text

ＴＨＥ ＷＩＤＥ ＬＯＯＫ•real fullwidth characters, not faked spacing

Why fullwidth instead of just adding spaces?

They look noticeably different, and the difference is the whole aesthetic. Fullwidth forms are real, separate Unicode characters — ａ is not a with padding, it is U+FF41 — designed to occupy one full character cell alongside Chinese, Japanese and Korean text. The spacing is even, the letterforms are drawn for that width, and nothing collapses. Inserting ordinary spaces gives you uneven gaps that line-wrap in the wrong places and get normalised away by some fields. Both are above; the difference is obvious once they are side by side.

Where does it work?

About as widely as fancy text gets. Any font with CJK support already has these characters, which is very nearly all of them on every modern device. The one thing to check is width, not support: every character is twice as wide, so a display name that fit comfortably may now be truncated mid-word. Paste it in and look before you save.

Where did the look come from?

From Japanese computing, by accident. Fullwidth forms exist so Latin letters can be set on the same grid as CJK characters, and 1980s and 90s Japanese software is full of them. Vaporwave borrowed the wide look wholesale as shorthand for that era of technology — more of that history in a short history of fancy text.
